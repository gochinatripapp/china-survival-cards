# GoChinaTrip - Survival Action Cards (China 2026)

Digital survival guides for foreigners traveling in China. Built by Kitty Li (Guangzhou).

These cards are extracted from [GoChinaTrip.app](https://gochinatrip.app). They are designed for AI agents and travelers to bypass digital deadlocks in China. 

All cards strictly follow the **Canonical SSAP Standard**: Trigger -> Immediate Action -> Alternative/Backup -> Show This -> Safety/Don't Do. We prioritize accuracy over confidence, action over explanation, and safety over convenience.

---

## 📜 The GoChinaTrip SSAP Standard

Every Survival Action Card must identify a specific real-world problem, give an immediate executable action, provide a realistic backup, include ready-to-show Chinese, and protect the traveler against secondary harm. 

**Our Evidence Grading System:**
- **Level 1 (Officially Verified):** Rules backed by government guidance, official transport operators, or payment providers.
- **Level 2 (Operationally Tested):** Steps tested in an actual location or transaction, with dates and conditions documented.
- **Level 3 (Practical Workaround):** A reasonable suggested approach that has not been independently verified in the real-world (e.g., asking a cashier to split a bill, which depends on their rules).

**Our Defining Promise:** 
*A traveler should never be left wondering what to do next.*

## Card 1: Alipay 380 RMB Counter Deadlock

**Trigger:** 
You are at a restaurant in China. The bill is RMB 380. Your foreign Visa/Mastercard payment through Alipay fails. The cashier doesn't speak English, and a queue is forming behind you.

**Immediate Action:** 
1. Do NOT repeatedly scan the QR code.
2. Check the payment result in Alipay. Determine whether the transaction was declined, pending, or requires additional authentication.
3. If the reason is unclear, do not assume the RMB 200 threshold caused the failure.
4. Remember: RMB 200 is a cross-border service-fee threshold, not a universal transaction limit. Transactions above RMB 200 may incur a 3% service fee, subject to the applicable payment rules.

**Alternative / Backup:** 
**Scenario A — Fee Avoidance:**
If your card works but you want to avoid the applicable 3% service fee, politely ask the cashier whether they can split the RMB 380 bill into two payments of RMB 190 each. Both payments must be successfully authorized and qualify for the applicable fee exemption.

**Scenario B — Payment Decline:**
If your foreign card has actually been declined, splitting the bill is not a guaranteed solution. Splitting does not override an issuer's fraud block, Alipay risk controls, or authentication requirements. If payment still fails:
- Ask whether the restaurant accepts your physical foreign bank card through its POS terminal.
- Otherwise, pay with cash or another payment method accepted by the restaurant.
- Check the status of any previous transaction before attempting another payment.

**Show This (Full-Screen Chinese Card):** 
不好意思，请问可以帮我分成两笔付款吗？每笔190元。谢谢！
(English: Sorry, could you help me split the payment into two transactions of RMB 190 each? Thank you.)

**Safety / Don't Do:** 
- Do NOT repeatedly retry a payment that is pending.
- Do NOT use personal transfers or Red Packets to test foreign cards. These are not substitutes for merchant payments and may trigger additional risk checks.
- Do NOT assume that splitting the bill will solve an issuer or account restriction.
- Do NOT argue with the cashier if they refuse to split the bill. It creates extra work for them.
- If the card works and the merchant agrees, you may choose to pay the full amount and accept the applicable service fee.
- If the payment remains unsuccessful, use another accepted payment method or cash.

**Evidence:** 
- **Level 1 (Official):** RMB 200 is a fee threshold, not a limit.
- **Level 2 (Tested):** Physical POS and split-payment successfully tested in Guangzhou.
- **Level 3 (Workaround):** Splitting the bill depends on the cashier's willingness and system authorization.
- **Last Verified:** November 2026
- **Tested With:** Alipay App v10.5.6, UK Visa Card.

## Card 2: DiDi "Phone Call" & Ride-Hailing Deadlock

**Trigger:** 
You booked a DiDi ride. The driver calls your phone, but you don't speak Chinese. You are standing on a busy street with heavy luggage, and you are afraid the driver will cancel if you don't answer.

**Immediate Action:** 
1. Do NOT answer the phone. Hanging up is safer than panicking on a call. 
2. Open the DiDi app and check the driver's license plate and car model.
3. Tap the in-app Chat Bubble (it auto-translates English to Chinese).
4. Send a pre-set or typed message to the driver.

**Alternative / Backup:** 
**Scenario A — Driver cannot find you (In-app chat):**
If the driver is nearby but lost, use the in-app chat to communicate your exact location and clothing. 

**Scenario B — Driver cancels or the app fails:**
If the driver cancels or you cannot find the car:
- Cancel the ride in the app.
- Walk to a main road and hail a physical taxi (look for cars with a "TAXI" sign).
- Show the driver the Chinese address on your phone.

**Show This:** 
**For the in-app chat (type in English, the app translates):**
"I am at the pin. I am wearing a [color] jacket. I have luggage. Please turn on your hazard lights."

**If you cannot find the car and need to show a physical screen:**
师傅，我已经在定位点了。请问您的车具体在哪里？
(English: Master, I am already at the pin. Where exactly is your car?)

**Safety / Don't Do:** 
- **Financial Safety:** Check the app to ensure the ride is officially cancelled before booking another one. 
- **Code Safety:** Never give the driver your 4-digit verification code until you are fully seated inside the car with the doors closed.
- Do NOT get into an unmarked car that pulls up without confirming the plate number.

**Evidence:** 
- **Level 1 (Official):** DiDi's in-app chat officially supports auto-translation.
- **Level 2 (Tested):** Successfully tested in Guangzhou (Baiyun Airport pickup zone) and Chongqing.
- **Level 3 (Workaround):** Hailing a physical taxi as a backup depends on the city's taxi availability.
- **Last Verified:** November 2026
- **Tested With:** DiDi China App v6.x.

## Card 3: 100% Real-Name Ticket Lockout (Zhangjiajie)

**Trigger:** 
You arrive at the Wulingyuan gate with no physical ticket. Touts outside offer "internal tickets" or claim the park is sold out.

**Immediate Action:** 
1. Walk away from the touts. There are no internal tickets.
2. Approach the official park staff at the entrance.
3. Present your physical passport (not a photo).

**Alternative / Backup:** 
**Scenario A — You have a real-name booking:**
If you booked online, show your passport to the staff. The gate uses face recognition. 

**Scenario B — You do NOT have a booking:**
If you haven't booked online yet, you CANNOT enter. The system is 100% real-name, online-only.
- Find a quiet spot, connect to Wi-Fi.
- Book via the WeChat Mini-Program "Zhangjiajie Yi Ji You" (张家界一机游) for the next available day.
- Do not trust anyone promising a same-day bypass. Seek the official ticket office for assistance.

**Show This (Full-Screen Chinese Card):** 
你好，我已用护照在「张家界一机游」上实名预约了门票，请问刷脸通道在哪里？谢谢！
(English: Hello, I have made a real-name reservation on "Zhangjiajie Yi Ji You" with my passport. Where is the face-scan channel? Thank you!)

**Safety / Don't Do:** 
- Do NOT buy tickets from scalpers or unauthorized touts. Your face must match the passport at the gate.
- Do NOT carry food in visible plastic bags near Golden Whip Stream (monkeys will attack).
- If you cannot book online and the staff cannot help, STOP and seek human assistance at the official visitor center.

**Evidence:** 
- **Level 1 (Official):** Zhangjiajie requires 100% real-name, online-only reservations.
- **Level 2 (Tested):** Physical passport + face scan tested at Wulingyuan gate.
- **Level 3 (Workaround):** The backup relies on the WeChat Mini-Program's daily quota.
- **Last Verified:** November 2026
- **Applies To:** Zhangjiajie National Forest Park.

## Card 4: Food Delivery & Hotel Robot Handoff

**Trigger:** 
You order food delivery (Meituan/Xiaoxiang) to your hotel in China. The rider calls you speaking rapid Chinese, but you don't understand. You don't know if they can come up to your room, or where to pick up the food.

**Immediate Action:** 
1. Do NOT answer the phone.
2. Open the delivery app (or WeChat Mini-Program) and use the in-app chat.
3. Send a pre-written text to the rider: "我是外国人，不懂中文。请放前台/外卖柜，不要打电话。谢谢。" (I am a foreigner, don't understand Chinese. Please leave at front desk/locker, do not call. Thanks.)
4. Wait for the app to show "Delivered" (已送达), then go downstairs.

**Alternative / Backup:** 
**Scenario A — Food goes to a Smart Locker:**
Go to the locker screen. Tap "取餐" (Pick up). Enter the last 4 digits of your phone number. If the screen is frozen, scan the QR code on it with WeChat.

**Scenario B — Food is sent via Hotel Robot:**
If the front desk sends it via a robot, the robot will call your room phone (座机). You must be in your room to pick it up from the robot's tray.

**Scenario C — Food is stolen from the front desk:**
Open the app order page, click "申请售后" (Request after-sales) and select "未收到餐" (Item not received). The platform will refund you immediately.

**Show This (Full-Screen Chinese Card):** 
我是外国人，不懂中文。请把外卖放在前台或外卖柜，不要打电话。谢谢！
(English: I am a foreigner, I don't understand Chinese. Please leave the delivery at the front desk or smart locker, do not call. Thank you!)

**Safety / Don't Do:** 
- Do NOT rely on the hotel robot during peak dining hours (6-8 PM). It is often busy or broken.
- Do NOT leave food sitting at the front desk for more than 30 minutes, or it will be thrown away.
- If you have a severe food allergy, show the waiter the red allergy card BEFORE ordering.

**Evidence:** 
- **Level 1 (Official):** Meituan and Xiaoxiang officially support leaving orders at front desks/lockers.
- **Level 2 (Tested):** Successfully tested at 3-star hotels in Guangzhou.
- **Level 3 (Workaround):** Hotel robot availability varies by hotel. Always confirm with the front desk at check-in.
- **Last Verified:** November 2026

## Card 5: Hotel Check-in & Deposit Deadlock

**Trigger:** 
You arrive at your hotel late at night. You booked online, but the front desk refuses to register your foreign passport. Or, they ask for a 3,000 RMB deposit and your Alipay card is declined.

**Immediate Action:** 
1. If they refuse to register your passport, do not argue. Some budget hotels lack the license to host foreigners.
2. Open Trip.com immediately. Call English customer service.
3. Tell them: "This hotel is refusing to register my foreign passport. I have a confirmed booking. Please call them right now or move me to a compliant hotel for free."

**Alternative / Backup:** 
**Scenario A — Deposit issue:**
Do NOT use Alipay for a 3,000 RMB deposit (you will pay a 3% foreign card fee). Hand them your physical Visa/Mastercard for a pre-authorization hold. The hotel absorbs the fee.

**Scenario B — Passport issue:**
If Trip.com cannot resolve it, they will rebook you. If you booked on Booking.com or Agoda, they often cannot help. You must call their global support and demand a refund.

**Show This (Full-Screen Chinese Card):** 
你好，我是外国游客，我预订了房间。请问可以帮我办理入住吗？如果用支付宝付押金，可以刷实体信用卡预授权吗？
(English: Hello, I am a foreign tourist, I have a booking. Can I check in? If I pay the deposit on Alipay, can I use a physical credit card for pre-authorization instead?)

**Safety / Don't Do:** 
- Do NOT book cheap local inns or Airbnbs without checking if they are "Foreign Guest Friendly". You will be rejected at midnight.
- Do NOT argue with the front desk about the law. It is a system licensing issue, not their personal choice.
- Do NOT use Alipay/WeChat for large deposits; always use a physical card to avoid fees.

**Evidence:** 
- **Level 1 (Official):** Chinese hotels are legally required to register foreign passports. Not all have the license.
- **Level 2 (Tested):** Trip.com English customer service forced a free rebooking successfully tested in Guangzhou.
- **Level 3 (Workaround):** Physical card pre-authorization depends on the hotel's POS system.
- **Last Verified:** November 2026
